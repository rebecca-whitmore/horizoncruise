# Horizon Cruise: brand foundation

**Central brand principle: Our expertise helps readers picture the experience and recognise whether it suits them.**

Build a UK cruise publication that helps readers choose a holiday that fits them, bringing together the ship, itinerary, time ashore, travel arrangements and overall value.

Newcomers and existing cruisers are equally important. Prioritise worldwide ocean cruise-and-stay holidays, with cruise-only itineraries a close second. Asia and Alaska are key opening destinations.

## 1. Starting audience

### Prioritise a decision, rather than a cruise category

Our readers share a question: **“What would this holiday be like, and would it suit me?”**

The needs connecting them are:

- **Fit:** Will the atmosphere, pace and experience suit me and my companions?
- **Confidence:** What do I need to understand to choose with confidence?
- **Value:** What does the complete holiday involve, and what is worth paying for?
- **Possibility:** What could I enjoy beyond the options I already know?

**Agreed audience direction:** Newcomers and existing cruisers have equal importance. A newcomer may need help picturing life on board; an experienced cruiser may want to explore another line, destination or style of holiday. Their experience changes the explanation they need, not their importance to us.

Cover discovery, enjoyment and traveller insight alongside practical decisions. Readers need not be ready to book to find value here. These are useful reader situations, not fixed personas or proven market segments.

### What the evidence changes

| Evidence | Implication—and limit |
| --- | --- |
| Iglu’s September 2024 survey of almost 5,000 customers reported itinerary as a key driver for 67%, value for 47%, and convenient travel for 42%. [1] | Support looking at the holiday beyond the ship. These are one retailer’s respondents, not representative UK market shares. |
| Cruise Trade News’ 2025 report surveyed 763 travellers, including 364 yet to cruise. It reported that 67% of that group lacked knowledge of fare inclusions. [2] | Cost clarity deserves a central place. Sampling and weighting are not established in the public summary; this does not prove the size of our audience. |
| Two UK-based forum contributors illustrate different uncertainties: a first-timer worried about extras despite being an experienced traveller; a Celebrity loyalist seeking pros and cons before trying Princess. [3][4] | Cruise experience does not remove uncertainty. These are individual accounts, not evidence of prevalence. |
| Existing publishers already combine planning help, inspiration and experience-led content. [5–8] | “Friendly expertise” is an expected quality, not an evidenced market gap. |

**Rebecca’s professional observation:** Agents and customers increasingly need help with tailor-made trips. Her B2B work in cruise-and-stay and complex itineraries gives this direction a credible knowledge base. We have not verified a market-wide shift towards tailor-made holidays.

### Deliberate boundaries

Do not fix an age, income or family profile without evidence, or turn each cruise type into an equal audience commitment.

**Agreed coverage priorities:**

- Ocean cruising only for now; river cruising is outside the opening scope.
- Worldwide cruise-and-stay holidays first, with cruise-only itineraries a close second.
- Asia and Alaska as key opening destinations.
- Luxury and higher-end options where relevant, without a luxury-only audience commitment.

Build selected, connected coverage across these priorities. Exhaustive coverage of every ship, port or sailing is not required.

## 2. Reader promise

> **We help you picture the cruise holiday experience and recognise whether it suits you—so you can discover possibilities and choose with confidence.**

Readers should consistently leave with one or more of these gains:

- A clearer sense of whether an experience suits their preferences.
- An understanding of a meaningful difference between options.
- A realistic view of costs, inclusions, pace or practical demands.
- A worthwhile holiday possibility they had not considered.

**Publication filter:** Identify the reader’s question or curiosity and what a piece helps them understand. Inspiration should make an experience tangible. News should explain a consequence for holidaymakers.

For example, explain what a hotel stay adds to a cruise, its effect on pace and what to check. Interpret relevant new-ship differences rather than reproducing launch claims.

We can fulfil this through researched explanations, transparent comparisons, expert interviews and properly attributed traveller accounts. We cannot promise universal first-hand testing, exhaustive ship reviews or the “best” holiday for everyone.

## 3. Positioning

### Internal positioning statement

**For UK holidaymakers discovering cruising or exploring their next cruise, Horizon Cruise is an editorial guide to finding the right fit across the whole holiday. It combines cruise sales knowledge, verified research and real travellers’ perspectives to help readers picture the experience, understand what it offers and recognise whether it suits them.**

### Why choose us alongside established sources?

The focused competitor review found substantial overlap:

| Source reviewed | Visible offer | Consequence for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cruise & Travel | Broad inspiration, reviews, advice and news; booking guidance and cruise-and-stay features already exist. [5][6] | Neither editorial polish nor cruise-and-stay coverage is exclusive territory. |
| Cruise Critic | First-timer guidance addressing cost and suitability, alongside reviews and community. [7] | We cannot claim to have discovered the need for reassurance or comparison. |
| Cruise Mummy | Practical planning advice, industry background and extensive personal cruise experience. [8] | Warmth and expertise already coexist. Frequent first-hand travel is not our strongest basis for competition. |

This is a focused page review, not a comprehensive audit.

**The credible reason to return:** Readers learn that we consistently make cruise holidays easier to picture and understand: the atmosphere, experiences, possibilities and how the parts fit together. Existing publications demonstrate that readers have established choices; we must earn our place through useful insight and consistent delivery. Research has not established an unserved niche.

Rebecca supplies practical sales knowledge; verified sources supply changing facts; travellers supply lived experience. Editorial judgment connects them to the reader’s decision.

This supports a future sales team: readers can learn to trust our judgment before seeking personal help. Commercial potential remains a hypothesis. Launch as a useful publication without a booking enquiry service.

Future affiliate links, sponsorships and sales relationships should be clearly identified. Recommendations must remain useful when the best answer earns us nothing.

Link to other cruise publications and sources when they add reader value. The SEO & Discovery Lead owns the detailed linking and backlink strategy.

## 4. Brand character

**A trusted, friendly guide with a discerning eye.** Knowledgeable, curious and candid; welcoming without forced familiarity.

| Quality | What the reader experiences |
| --- | --- |
| Editorial credibility | Facts are checked and dated where needed. Recommendations explain their basis. Research, personal experience and commercial material are distinguishable. |
| Warmth | Ordinary questions receive respectful answers. Jargon is explained. Different budgets and preferences are treated without snobbery. |
| Personal relevance | We describe the conditions under which something suits a reader, rather than declaring it perfect for everyone. |
| Enjoyment and discovery | Specific scenes, details and traveller voices help readers picture a holiday and feel interested in exploring further. |

### Public voice in practice

Illustrative wording, not verified product advice:

- **Practical:** “Check whether the fare includes transfers, gratuities and the hotel stay. These can change the comparison.”
- **Discerning:** “An overnight stay gives you more time ashore. Whether that improves the holiday depends on what you want to do there.”
- **Warm:** “Enjoying your usual cruise line is a useful starting point. Identify what you want to keep—and what you would like to change.”

Be direct with specifications and costs; allow more texture in features and traveller stories. Use British English. Avoid unsupported superlatives, assumed intimacy and sales urgency.

Lead with experience and suitability. Explain relevant limitations where they exist, without assuming every holiday involves a compromise. Publish valuable, real insight whether or not it promotes a product.

### Brand-led, with accountable expertise

Rebecca need not become the public personality. Explain editorial responsibility and sources honestly.

Use publication bylines for desk research, source notes and an editorial contact. Identify contributors and their relevant experience where agreed. Substantiate professional expertise.

Never present desk research as first-hand experience, invent an expert persona or imply a larger staff exists.

## Decisions and next ownership

The approved brand name is **Horizon Cruise**, with **horizoncruise.co.uk** as the domain. Use this name in future TL work.

The foundation decisions have been supplied: equal importance for newcomers and existing cruisers; ocean cruise-and-stay first, cruise-only a close second; Asia and Alaska opening priorities; brand-led publishing; and the central principle of picturing the experience and recognising suitability. No further foundation decision is needed before the TLs begin useful work.

**Recommended editorial application:** Use a sailing as a starting point when it offers a useful story, rather than requiring one article per sailing or a separate article for every component. An Alaska cruise with a Vancouver stay might support an anchor feature about the experience and suitability, a reusable Vancouver stay article, and an explanation of Alaska route differences. These are illustrative ideas, not commissioned articles.

Develop connected coverage across Asia and Alaska to balance depth and range. Give ships, ports, flights and excursions standalone articles when they have a clear reader purpose and sufficient substance. Describe researched features honestly; reserve first-hand reviews for actual contributor experience.

**Editorial Director:** Own the opening story mix and depth of coverage. **SEO & Discovery Lead:** Inform search intent, connections between articles and linking strategy. **Launch Director:** Sequence the work. The Brand Director maintains coherence with this foundation and synthesises audience evidence.
